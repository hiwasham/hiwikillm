# Andrej Karpathy About Page

> Andrej Karpathy’s personal about page summarizes his AI research, teaching, roles at OpenAI and Tesla, major projects, talks, writing, and publications.

## TL;DR

- Andrej Karpathy describes himself as someone who likes “to train deep neural nets on large datasets.”
- Since 2024, he has focused on educational AI videos on YouTube, split into a technical track and a general-audience track.
- In 2023–2024, he returned to OpenAI and built a new team working on midtraining and synthetic data generation.
- From 2017–2022, he was Director of AI at Tesla, leading the computer vision team for Tesla Autopilot and briefly Tesla Optimus.
- During his Stanford PhD, he worked on convolutional/recurrent neural networks for computer vision, NLP, and their intersection; he designed and taught Stanford’s first deep learning course, CS231n.
- His notable projects include micrograd, char-rnn, arxiv-sanity, neuraltalk2, ConvNetJS, and ulogme.
- His publication record includes image captioning, visual-semantic alignments, ImageNet, video classification, PixelCNN++, World of Bits, and simulated robotics/control work.

- Pet projects:
  - micrograd: “a tiny scalar-valued autograd engine”; implements backpropagation/reverse-mode autodiff over a dynamically built DAG plus a small neural-network library with PyTorch-like API.
  - char-rnn: Torch character-level language model using LSTMs/GRUs/RNNs.
  - arxiv-sanity: tool to tame “the overwhelming flood of papers on Arxiv”; supports discovery, search/sort by similarity, recent/popular papers, and recommendations; deployed at arxiv-sanity.com.
  - arxiv-sanity-lite: “from-scratch rewrite” described as “much better.”
  - neuraltalk2: early image-captioning project in Torch; later extended with Justin Johnson to dense captioning.
  - ConvNetJS: deep learning library written from scratch in JavaScript, enabling web demos that train CNNs or ordinary networks in the browser.
  - ulogme: privacy-first productivity/time tracker written because he disliked RescueTime uploading private computer usage statistics to the cloud.
